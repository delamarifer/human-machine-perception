Course Overview

Computer vision has long taken its questions from the study of human vision, and this course teaches the two together. It starts with images as arrays and linear filtering, relating a bank of filters to the receptive fields of early visual cortex, then covers convolutional networks for recognition, Vision Transformers and attention, and learning visual representations without labels through contrastive methods and CLIP. Later sessions cover dataset quality and model evaluation, adapting pretrained models through fine-tuning and distillation, and generative models. Throughout, models are tested against perceptual phenomena such as metamers, the shape–texture bias and the Thatcher illusion. Students finish with an original project: either a working demo of a new application that combines ideas from the course, or an experiment that measures the limitations of current artificial vision systems.

The course combines lectures and discussions with hands-on experimentation. Each session introduces new concepts and gives students opportunities to apply them through guided coding labs. Homework assignments reinforce learning and extend lab activities.

Students are encouraged to experiment, ask questions, explore their own interests, and critically evaluate how computer vision systems work and where they fail.

Format

The course runs for three weeks, 15 sessions, from 2:00 to 4:00 pm. Each day mixes a short quiz or discussion, lectures, and hands-on labs in Google Colab, so bring a laptop and a Google account. You are assessed on a daily quiz or discussion, two assignments and a final project; see Grading.

Final Project

Students design and build an original computer vision application using concepts and techniques from the course, in one of two tracks: build a demo, or run an experiment. The research question, the deliverables and how to hand in are on Assignments & Projects; templates for each step are on project templates.
