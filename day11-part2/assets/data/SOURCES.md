# `day11/assets/data` — where every image came from

Rule for this part: **no figure of Malik's, and no figure from anyone else's paper.** Everything here is
either a freely licensed photograph with its licence recorded below, or it is drawn in `parts/data.js` as
inline SVG. Videos are **embedded** from the authors' own channels, which is linking, not copying.

## Photographs used on slides

### `teleop-robonaut2-iss.jpg`
- **Used on:** "Someone has to be the robot" (the teleoperation slide).
- **Source file:** [File:Tom Marshburn teleoperating R2.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tom_Marshburn_teleoperating_R2.jpg)
  on Wikimedia Commons.
- **Original:** NASA, image ISS035-E-030791, 25 April 2013. NASA astronaut Tom Marshburn, Expedition 35
  Flight Engineer, in a Taskboard and Tele-operations session with Robonaut 2 aboard the International
  Space Station.
- **Licence:** **Public domain** (work of NASA). No permission needed, no attribution required; the slide
  credits NASA anyway.
- **Change made here:** resized from 4288x2848 to 1600x1062. No crop, no retouching, no recolouring.
- **Honesty note, which the slide itself carries:** this is *not* a manipulation-data-collection rig. It is
  an astronaut teleoperating a humanoid on the ISS. It is on the slide because it shows the shape of the
  thing — a person wearing a head-mounted display, hands in controllers, with the robot's hands moving in
  front of them — and not because it is one of the rigs Malik is describing. The caption on the slide says
  this out loud so nobody reads it as a photograph of a data farm.

## Looked for and not found under a reusable licence

A photograph of **someone wearing a head-mounted camera**, or a real **first-person frame of two hands
doing a kitchen task**, for the videos-of-humans slide. Wikimedia Commons has nothing usable: its search
for head-mounted and wearable cameras returns body-worn police cameras and television remote controls.
Epic-Kitchens frames are **CC BY-NC**, which is a licence this repository has avoided. So that slide shows
the project's own video instead, and the two problems with human video are **drawn**, not photographed.
