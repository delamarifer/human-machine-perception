## Navigation: GOAT

- Paper: GOAT: GO to Any Thing. Matthew Chang, Theophile Gervet, Mukul Khanna, Sriram Yenamandra,
  Dhruv Shah, So Yeon Min, Kavit Shah, Chris Paxton, Saurabh Gupta, Dhruv Batra, Roozbeh Mottaghi,
  Jitendra Malik, Devendra Singh Chaplot. Robotics: Science and Systems (RSS) 2024, Delft.
  arXiv:2311.06430, https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06430
- Numbers used on the slides, all from the paper abstract: over 90 hours in 9 homes, 675 goals,
  more than 200 object instances, 83% overall success, rising from about 60% on the first goal in a
  home to about 90% after exploration.
- **No official project page or demo video found.** Searched on 2026-11-16: the arXiv abstract page
  lists no project URL, and a web search turned up only the paper, the proceedings PDF and the separate
  GOAT-Bench repository (github.com/Ram81/goat-bench). Nothing unofficial has been substituted. The
  navigation slides use a drawn floor-plan figure instead. If Fernanda knows of an official video, it
  can be embedded on slide 2 the way the locomotion video is on slide 4.

## Numbers that are Malik's own words, not measurements

- "a child hears about a million words a month", "about 100 million words by the age of ten",
  "close to a trillion" for a language model, and the 10,000x ratio: his arithmetic, said out loud in
  the talk (around 6:17-7:44). It is an order-of-magnitude claim on a slide, not a figure from a study.
  Every slide that carries it says so.
- The 29% slope and the 41% street: his own numbers from the talk (around 4:08-6:01). The slope figure
  is drawn to those two grades at their true angles.
- "navigation nearly done, locomotion good progress, manipulation a long way to go": his judgement,
  offered in the talk as something that will probably offend people. Not a benchmark.
