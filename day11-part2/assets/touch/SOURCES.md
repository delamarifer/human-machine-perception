**A note on the second file, and Fernanda's decision.** Commons tags it "copyrighted free use" rather than
giving a named licence, and the tag itself carries a warning to verify at the source. Adelson's own page
grants use for non-commercial educational purposes, which a course deck is. **Fernanda was shown this and
chose to keep the image (2026-11-16).** If it ever has to come out, drop `checkershadow-proof.svg`, remove the
second figure from the "The same person, twice" slide in `../../parts/touch.js` along with its note passage,
and say the proof aloud instead; the first image is cleanly CC BY-SA 4.0 and can stay.

## What is NOT claimed on this slide

**Malik does not quote Ted Adelson in the talk.** At 37:59 he says the tactile sensor design tradition goes
back to GelSight, and GelSight is Adelson's (with Micah Kimo Johnson and others at MIT). That is the only
link in the talk itself. Everything the slide draws from there — that the same person made the checkershadow
illusion and the tactile sensor, and what that rhymes with — is **our connection, not his**, and the slide
and the notes both say so.
