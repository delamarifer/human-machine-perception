Week 1 · Assignment 1

Are vision models still blind?

In class we saw Vision Language Models Are Blind: questions a child can answer, such as whether two circles touch or how many times two lines cross, that the best models of 2024 often got wrong. Someone asked how newer models do. That is this assignment. You test models that came out since, then make new images that no model has seen, and find where they break.

How it works

Part A: replicate. Everyone runs the same test on a newer model, with the starter notebook, and compares the result with the paper's.

Part B: fresh images. The test has been public since 2024, so a newer model may have seen it in training. A high score could mean it can see, or that it memorized the test. You generate new images with code, change one thing step by step, and find where the models break.

Part C: reflection and sources. What you found, and a log of every source you used.

Using the internet and AI tools is allowed, and part of the point. Finding models, reading model cards and adapting other people's code is how this work is done. Two rules: log every source in Part C, and be able to explain every line you hand in. You work alone, but you can talk about ideas with anyone.

Before you run something, write down what you expect. A wrong prediction loses no points; a missing one does.

The test, and what it found in 2024

Part A · Replicate 35 points

  1. Look, then predict 5 pts

    The notebook shows one image from each task. Answer them yourself. Then pick the model you will test, and predict: which task will it find hardest, and why?

  2. Run a newer model 10 pts

    Run your model on the course subset: 20 images from each of the 9 tasks, the same for everyone, with the paper's own questions. The notebook scores the answers and makes a table of accuracy by task.

  3. Compare with 2024 10 pts

    Plot your model's accuracy next to the paper's models.

    Which tasks improved, and which did not? Is your model better than the best model of 2024? With 20 images per task, how big a difference would you trust?

  4. Look at the mistakes 10 pts

    Show at least six images your model got wrong, with its answers.

    Is there a pattern: counts off by one, the same answer every time, trouble with small or thin things? Compare each task with the score you would get by always giving the most common answer.

Part B · Fresh images 45 points

Pick one task. Write code that draws new images for it, with the right answer known, and change one thing step by step: the gap between the circles, the line width, the number of crossings, the image size, the colors, the angle. The notebook has two example drawing functions (two circles, and two lines that cross) that you can change, or you can write your own or adapt code you find.

  1. Question and prediction 8 pts

    One sentence: what you change, and what you expect to happen to accuracy, with a reason. Written before you run anything.

  2. Make the images 10 pts

    At least 5 levels of the thing you change, and at least 10 images per level. Show a row of examples, one per level.

  3. Run two conditions 10 pts

    Run the same images in at least two conditions: two models (for example your open model and Gemini, or a small and a larger version), or one model with two ways of asking.

  4. The human side 7 pts

    Answer a sample of the images yourself, and ask at least one other person. Keep it informal: no names or personal details in what you hand in.

  5. The result 10 pts

    A plot of accuracy against the thing you changed, one line per condition, with people marked.

    Where does each model break? Did the fresh images give the same picture as BlindTest? One thing that surprised you, and the limits of your test.

Part C · Reflection and sources 20 points

Reflection (half a page, 12 points). Are vision models still blind? Answer from your results in Parts A and B, and say what your test cannot tell apart (for example memorizing versus seeing). End with one thing you would test next. It can become your project.

Source log (8 points). A table with one row per source (models, datasets, code, tutorials, forum answers): what it is, the link, how you used it, and what you changed. Then one line on any AI tools you used and what for.
